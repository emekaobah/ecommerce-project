# Oja — Design

Status: agreed 2026-09-26. Product name "Oja" (kept in a single config constant).

## Product

- Demo-grade (production-hardenable) **multi-vendor marketplace** on **Mercur.js v2.3.5** (Medusa 2.20.1).
- Launch market: Nigeria, NGN, English. Architecture is **multi-region ready** (see Multi-country).
- Seed: 3 Lagos vendors (grocery, fashion, electronics) in different areas; Paystack + Shipbubble test keys.

## Repo

Private GitHub monorepo (`emekaobah/ecommerce-project`) scaffolded by `create-mercur-app` (Bun). Pin Mercur/Medusa versions; verify a fresh scaffold boots.

- `apps/api` — Mercur backend (+ our custom modules)
- Mercur admin panel and vendor panel (web)
- Mercur Next.js storefront — kept as reference only
- `apps/mobile-buyer`, `apps/mobile-vendor` — Expo, Expo Router, dev builds
- `packages/*` — shared API client, types, design tokens/UI

Visual direction: **Adire** (chosen 2026-10-20 in the redesign; supersedes the earlier Monochrome direction). all 40 buyer and vendor screens in the chosen direction are in `docs/redesign/oja-adire-screens.html`. Deep adire indigo is the brand surface and primary button; the adire resist-dye pattern appears only in brand moments (sign-in, the Home band, shop and order headers, pickup codes, confirmations, vendor headers); everything else is white with indigo-tinted tiles. Shops lead (cover photo, monogram, rating, open state). One primary button per screen, with the price in the label when money moves. Token roles: indigo `#1B2559` brand, primary buttons, selection, ratings; dye `#2E3D8F` pattern and links; mist `#EEF0F7` photo tiles and secondary buttons; sun `#F2B33D` only for deals, countdowns and progress; leaf `#1E7A4C` open, done and picked, at chip, pill and badge size only; ember `#B4531A` short items, deadlines under 4 hours and needs-attention; red `#B42318` only for reject, cancel and destructive actions; ink `#12162B`, muted `#5B6178`, lines `#DDE1EE`. Type: Bricolage Grotesque for headings and prices, Manrope for everything else. Prices are whole naira (`₦14,500`), and dates beat durations ("Arrives Thu 23 Oct"). Status chips always pair a symbol and label with colour. Spacing sits on a 4pt grid (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48): 16 gutter; bands 8 top, 20 bottom, with straight edges and nothing overlapping them; sections 24 above their heading and 12 below; 12 inside a group, 20–24 between groups. Type: 11 sizes and four weights (400, 600, 700, 800). Display, in Bricolage Grotesque: 44 pickup digits, 34 takeover, 28 confirmations, 26 tab root titles, 22 a screen named after a thing (shop, product) and band headlines, 20 pushed-screen titles, 18 section headings and totals. Text, in Manrope: 14 names, prices and buttons, 13 body, 11.5 meta, 10.5 labels and chips. Corners: 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 and full. Buttons: 52 sticky main action, 48 standard, 44 in a list row. One title per screen; emphasis runs title, primary action, section heading, row title, meta (audited and snapped 2026-10-21). Every full-screen surface and button is indigo or white; green, ember and red never fill a screen (red also marks destructive buttons). The pattern always fades out behind text so letters never sit on its strokes. Kept from the Market Day direction: a full-screen new-order takeover with sound, redrawn in indigo with a pulsing sun accept clock; quantity pills on the pick list, the sun "10% off at this shop" deal badge beside crossed-out sale prices. The redesign is presentation only: every screen uses data and actions the apps and backend already have. Home is the existing listings feed, grouped by shop and nearest first. There's no deals feed or countdown, because sale end dates don't reach the buyer. Shops and categories have no images, search has no filters or sort, and reviews show only an average and count. **Dark mode** follows the system setting, with no in-app toggle in v1:
- Surfaces are neutral charcoal with no brand tint (decided 2026-10-20, after an indigo-tinted version was rejected as generic): page `#111113`, cards and inputs `#1C1C1F`, tiles `#26262A`, lines `#333338`, text `#F2F2F4`, secondary `#B4B4BC`.
- The brand band keeps `#1B2559`. Primary buttons lighten to `#3D4DBB` with white text. Links and accents are `#AEB8FF`. Indigo keeps its light-mode roles only: band, buttons, selection, links.
- Status chips use deep fills with light text.
- The pickup QR stays black on white.

**Screen states** (drawn in the States section of `docs/redesign/oja-adire-screens.html`):
- **Loading:** skeletons in the shape of the content, never a blank screen or a lone spinner. What the phone already knows (address, greeting, shop name, back button) shows at once. A spinner only for waits with no layout: confirming a payment, opening Paystack. The shimmer stops under Reduce Motion.
- **Refreshing:** pull to refresh keeps the current list; a failed refresh shows a toast, not an error screen.
- **Errors:** a quiet mist emblem, the plain-language title the code already uses ("Couldn't load your orders"), what is safe ("You haven't been charged", "Nothing you've picked is lost") and Try again. Raw error messages go to logs, not the screen; today `ErrorState` shows `error.message`, which the build should replace.
- **Empty:** the indigo pattern emblem, what will appear here, and at most two ways forward.
- **Inline problems** (payment not completed, order not placed and refunded, address not saved, price changed) sit above the button they affect in the ember note, announced to screen readers.

**Confirm steps** (drawn 2026-10-21): a confirm is a bottom sheet in the app's own style, never a system alert: a title that asks the question, one line of consequence, then the action (red when it destroys something) and Cancel. Used where an action can't be undone or moves money: deleting an address, pausing while orders are waiting (this replaces the warning inside the Pause sheet), handing a pickup order over (who is collecting), sending a refund to a bank account, and **Handed to courier** (new: today the app records the tap straight away). Not used where speed matters or the action is reversible: accept, add to cart, sign out (the cart stays with the account). Guests see a sign-in prompt on the Orders and Account tabs, nothing else is gated until checkout.

**Accessibility** targets **WCAG 2.2 AAA** in both themes (decided 2026-10-20), audited in `docs/redesign/oja-adire-screens.html`.
- **Contrast:** every text pair is at least 7:1, and every control outline at least 3:1. AAA colours:
  - Light: secondary and placeholder text `#494F66`; "Open" text `#165A3C`; ember text `#84391A`; red text `#962218`; New chip `#714800`.
  - Fills with white text, both themes: leaf `#186441`, ember `#90411A`, red `#AA2318`.
  - Dark: secondary and placeholder `#B4B4BC`; ember text `#F0A474`; red text `#F2938A`; primary button `#3D4DBB`; selected background `#2A2A30`.
  - Control outlines: `#80869E` light, `#7A7A83` dark.
- **Touch targets:** at least 44pt hit area. The add button on product tiles is drawn at about 36pt and get the 44pt hit area in code via `hitSlop`.
- **Interruptions:** the new-order takeover has "Not now" (2.2.4), and a phone-only setting turns it into a banner (3.2.5). This is the only addition AAA required, and it needs no backend change.
- **Focus:** focused fields get a 2px outline.
- **Paragraphs:** 1.5 line height.
- **Gestures:** every action has a non-gesture route.
- **Confirmation:** paying, rejecting, cancelling, marking short and handover each get a confirm or review step.
- **Signals:** colour is never the only signal, and the alert pairs sound with a haptic.
- **Text size:** text scales to 200%.
- **Screen readers:** icon-only buttons get spoken labels. Vendor app uses the same system at larger sizes with large touch targets.

## Buyer app

- Auth: **Clerk** (`@clerk/expo`) — native Google + Apple (required by App Store 4.8) + email. Clerk is the sole customer identity; Medusa customer emailpass disabled.
- Browse by category/seller, search. **Listings and product pages are shop-scoped** — a product is always shown as one shop's offer (price, delivery time, shop rating). **No cross-shop price comparison** (shops object; not in v1). The product page uses that space for summary, details, reviews and "more from this shop" suggestions.
- Multi-seller cart; checkout picks, **per seller**, either a Shipbubble rate (cheapest / fastest / best value, default best value) or **in-store pickup** where that shop offers it (see Pickup). A checkout where every shop is pickup asks for no delivery address, only the buyer's name and phone.
  - The Cart tab reads the cart grouped by shop (`GET /store/carts/:id/sellers`). The cart id is kept on the device, one for the guest and one per buyer; a guest cart with items carries over on sign-in (`@oja/domain` `cartOnSignIn`), and signing out leaves the buyer's cart with their account.
  - **Each line is pinned to its own offer's price** (#15). Medusa prices cart lines per variant and Mercur's cart pricing hooks put every offer in the cart into one context, so with two shops' offers of one variant in a cart, a quantity change, sign-in or address change repriced both at the cheaper shop's price. Our add and update-quantity routes price the line from its offer and store it as a custom price, which Medusa's refreshes keep. A shop's price change reaches a line when its quantity next changes, so **checkout must reprice every line from its offer before payment**. It does, and when a price changed the buyer is shown which lines changed and the new total before any payment starts (#57).
  - Prices are VAT-inclusive through the **currency's** tax-inclusive preference: offer prices are per currency, and Medusa applies a region's preference only to region-scoped prices.
- Payment (#16, ADR 0003): checkout reprices every line from its offer, sets the address and each shop's delivery (Shipbubble, #23), and the backend initialises a Paystack transaction (our `paystack` payment provider; the `medusa-payment-paystack` plugin captured without a webhook). The app opens `authorization_url` in a WebView and catches Paystack's redirect to its deep link; the order is placed only when Paystack's signed `charge.success` webhook arrives, which captures the payment and splits the cart into one order per shop. A cancelled or failed payment places nothing; paying again starts a new transaction.
  - Built (#38, ADR 0003): a paid cart that fails to become orders (a code's last use taken, stock sold out, an offer or delivery gone) is refunded in full, never placed in part. Once Mercur's split of the cart has finished, our webhook subscriber tries it once more, then refunds the payment holding the cart's lock, fixes the cart (sold-out lines cut, gone lines and deliveries removed, used-up codes dropped) and keeps why on it. `GET /store/carts/:id/checkout` returns `not_placed: { reason, amount, changes }`; the buyer is pushed and emailed why and what was refunded, the app goes back to checkout to show what changed, and paying the new total clears it. A charge for a different amount than its session is refunded in full too.
  - **Cart layout** (redesign, 2026-10-21): grouped by shop, Etsy-style. Each shop ends with a mist `#EEF0F7` subtotal block (dark: `#26262A`): "Subtotal" and the amount, then "n items + delivery" and a visible "Checkout this shop" link (only when the cart holds more than one shop; it is Pay for this shop only, below). The main button names the count: "Checkout 2 shops · ₦47,700". Not item-level or multi-shop selection: the shop cart takes one whole shop.
  - **Pay for this shop only** (#11, `src/lib/shop-cart.ts`, `src/workflows/checkout/shop-cart.ts`): beside the combined checkout, the cart offers each shop's items paid on their own. `POST /store/carts/:id/shop-cart` (`{ seller_id }`, the signed-in buyer's own cart) moves the shop's items through add-to-cart into a new **shop cart** of theirs, with the codes that follow them (ADR 0010), and returns it by shop. That cart goes through the same checkout and becomes an order group with one order. The original cart keeps the other shops' items and stays the app's cart. It loses the shop's delivery and its Paystack session, so an old page for the whole cart places nothing and is refunded in full if paid (ADR 0003). A cart holding one shop, or already paid, is refused. `POST /store/carts/:id/shop-cart/back` (`{ shop_cart_id }`) moves a shop cart's items and codes back to the cart they came from and empties it; its old Paystack page is then refunded if paid, and a shop cart already paid is refused (409). Each move is one workflow under the cart lock Mercur's completion takes, undone if it fails part-way. A code left on both carts counts one use. In the app each shop's group in a cart of several shops has "Pay for this shop only" under its subtotal, beside the combined Checkout. It opens checkout on the shop cart (`/checkout?cartId=`), and going back from it without paying moves the items back. Paying it leaves the app's cart in place.
- Order history showing the parent order group → per-seller orders; live tracking.
  - **Orders list layout** (chosen 2026-10-21, not built): two tabs, **Ongoing** (anything not finished, including orders waiting on the buyer; a split checkout until every shop is done) and **Past** (delivered, collected, rejected, cancelled, refunded, with Order again and search by item), each with a count; it opens on Ongoing when anything is in progress, otherwise Past. Depends on `GET /store/order-history` gaining `state=active|past` (Ongoing unpaged) and `q` for search; today it takes only `limit`/`offset` and neither Mercur's `/store/order-groups` nor Medusa's `/store/orders` can filter by Oja's states. Until then the app keeps one newest-first list with status chips.
  - **Order again** (designed 2026-10-21, not built; the build is a separate scope): a delivered or collected shop order offers Order again on the order and as a pill on its Orders row. It opens a sheet, not a screen: each item ticked at today's price, a changed price called out ("Now ₦3,800 each, was ₦3,500"), anything unavailable greyed and unticked, then "Add 3 items to cart" with the total. It adds to the cart (quantities merge) and never skips checkout. A shop outside its hours still takes the order, prepared when it opens; a paused shop, a shop closed for a date, or one that no longer delivers to the buyer's address offers no button ("Closed until Tue 14 Oct"). Per shop, because each shop delivers on its own. No endpoint exists for it yet.
  - The Orders tab (#17) reads `GET /store/order-history` (the buyer's order groups, newest first) and `GET /store/order-history/:id` (`src/lib/buyer-orders.ts`), not Mercur's `/store/order-groups`, which has no per-order state or refund. Each shop's order carries its `fulfilment` (as staff see it), `paid_total` (what was captured for it, which neither a refund's credit line nor an order edit changes; an order nothing was captured for reads `total + credit_line_total`), each line's `unavailable_quantity` (removed as unavailable while picking, #22) and `refund` (its `order_refund` rows summed; a failed one stops counting once made again). `@oja/domain` turns these into what the buyer reads: "Not accepted in time" for an order the deadline rejected, else the shop's reason; "Refund on its way", "Refunded" or "Refund delayed". A group of one order (#65) is shown as that order.
- Saved addresses, public reviews + ratings, product wishlist, promo codes.
  - **Promotions** (#6, ADR 0010): codes are entered at checkout (`POST /store/carts/:id/checkout/promo-codes`, `DELETE …/:code`), and a code that can't be used says why (not valid, expired, not on yet, used up, not for anything in the cart). Shops create theirs in the web vendor panel; the operator creates marketplace-wide ones in the admin panel. A cart's promotions are worked out shop by shop (`applyShopPromotionsWorkflow`) after every change and again before payment: a shop's on its own items alone, the operator's on the whole cart, and only the operator's discount delivery. Checkout shows each line's discounts by code and each promotion's shop ("On Lagos Grocery's items only"). Listings and the product page carry a badge for the listing's shop's running automatic promotions that apply to the product (`deals`, e.g. "10% off at this shop"), never another shop's.
- **Address entry** (ADR 0002): Google autocomplete or "use my current location" pre-fills the per-country form, then the buyer confirms a map pin; the pin's coordinates are validated with Shipbubble. Optional "Landmark / directions for the rider" field. Lookups go through the backend (`AddressLookupProvider`), signed-in only.

## Vendor app (fulfilment only)

- Auth: Mercur member (`/auth/member/emailpass`) + `x-seller-id`. Enable Medusa `rbac` flag.
  - Owner/manager → `role_seller_administration`; fulfilment staff → `role_seller_order_management`. Staff may quick-add new offers (ADR 0006) but never change existing ones.
  - Staff invited by owner from the web vendor panel.
- Catalogue, onboarding, promotions and review replies remain in the **web vendor panel**.
- **Pick and pack** (#21): Start picking opens a checklist of the order's lines. Staff tick a line off by hand or scan barcodes with the camera: a scan ticks one more of the line whose variant carries that barcode, EAN or UPC (GTINs compared as 14 digits, so a UPC-A read as EAN-13 still matches); a barcode on no line warns (`scanBarcode`, `@oja/domain`). Each line's picked count is kept on the order's fulfilment row (`picks`), so progress survives leaving the screen and shows on any phone. Mark packed is refused until every line is picked in full; partial availability (#22) adds "unavailable" as the other way to resolve a line. Vendor API: `POST /vendor/orders/:id/start-picking`, `GET /vendor/orders/:id/pick-list`, `POST /vendor/orders/:id/pick-list/:item_id` (`{ picked }` sets a tapped line; `{ add }` counts a scan, so two phones scanning at once both count), `POST /vendor/orders/:id/pack`.
- **Partial availability** (#22): on the checklist staff mark how many of a line's unpicked units the shop doesn't have (`POST /vendor/orders/:id/pick-list/:item_id/unavailable`, `{ quantity }`, none clears it). Marks are kept on the fulfilment row (`unavailable`); the first moves Picking → Partially available, clearing the last moves it back. The pick list carries each line's `unavailable` and `unavailable_amount` (each marked unit at its share of its line's total). Packing is refused while units are marked. `POST /vendor/orders/:id/remove-unavailable` shows staff a refund summary first in the app, then edits the order with the workflows behind Mercur's `/vendor/order-edits` routes (begin, set each line's quantity, request, Mercur's confirm, so totals, reservations and commission follow), records the units as `removed`, moves it back to Picking, and refunds exactly what the edit took off the order's total (`refundOwedWorkflow`). Refused with nothing marked, and when every item is marked unavailable: the pick list's `progress.allUnavailable` is true, and the refusal (400) carries `code: "cancel_instead"`, so the app offers to cancel the order instead (#17). A refund Paystack turns down leaves the edit standing and the order owing the buyer, and is retried (#16).
- **Cancelling an accepted order** (#17, decisions grilled 2026-10-17): from Accepted, Picking, Partially available, Packed (before a courier label is booked; a label Shipbubble cancelled leaves it Packed again) or Ready for pickup (a pickup order's Packed), the shop's owners and managers (`seller:update`, as for pausing the shop; fulfilment staff get 403) cancel a seller order with a reason the buyer is shown: Items unavailable, Shop can't fulfil this order, Buyer asked to cancel, or Other with a note (`CANCEL_REASONS`, `cancelReasonText`, `@oja/domain`; trigger `cancel`, state `cancelled` carrying `cancellation` { by, actor, at, reason, note }). Oja's operators cancel too, from the admin's order page (Cancel order widget). Routes: `GET|POST /vendor/orders/:id/cancel` and `GET|POST /admin/orders/:id/cancel`; the GET says whether it can be cancelled and what it refunds (`{ cancel: { allowed, refund_amount, currency_code } }`), the POST takes `{ reason, note? }` and answers `{ fulfilment, refund_amount, refund }`. They replace Mercur's and Medusa's cancel routes, whose `cancelOrderWorkflow` refunds every payment linked to the order in full: a combined checkout's whole Paystack payment. `cancelAcceptedOrderWorkflow` releases the order's stock reservations, cancels the order and gives back promo code uses (below); `cancelAndRefund` then refunds all that is left of the order's share (`cancelRefundOf`: its captures less every refund of it made, so items and its delivery fee, net of discounts, less what partial availability refunded, less what Oja's team settled or sends by hand; the GET, the POST's `refund_amount` and the buyer's push and email all give this amount) through the owed-refund path (`refundOwedWorkflow`, covering `all`): a cancelled order owes its buyer that share until refunded, a refund Paystack refuses is retried and handed to Oja's team like any owed refund (#16), and the other shops' orders in the checkout are untouched. A cancelled order (or a rejected one) pays its shop nothing (`payoutOf`: the buyer keeps none of its lines). A promo code's use goes back (`revertUsage`, with what its orders took off for a spend budget) only once every order it discounted is cancelled or rejected, counting the orders of a checkout sharing the code (a cart and its shop cart, ADR 0010), under the locks of the order's cart and of each cart sharing a code with it; when it goes back, the carts share the code no more, so a sharing cart paid for later counts a use of its own. `order_fulfilment.cancelled` (`{ id, amount }`) tells the buyer by push and email who cancelled it, why, and what is refunded (`orderCancelledPush`, `OrderCancelledEmail`). Vendor and buyer order responses carry `fulfilment.cancellation` ({ by, reason, note, at }); the buyer's order also carries its `refund` and `owed_refund` as before, and reads "Cancelled by the shop" (or "Cancelled by Oja's team", `cancelledBy`) with the reason (`orderStatus`). The web panels' built-in Cancel actions send no reason, so the routes refuse them (400); like the Store closure box (#21), the button stays until the panels' leftover controls are dealt with before go-live. Operators use the Cancel order widget, shops the vendor app. In the vendor app, owners and managers (`useMayManageShop`) get Cancel order on the order screen while `canCancel`: a reason picker (`cancelRequest` makes the body, Other needs a note), the refund from the GET, then whether the refund is on its way or pending (refused, so retried). When every item is unavailable the pick list and the refund summary offer Cancel order with Items unavailable picked instead of removing them; fulfilment staff are told to ask an owner or manager. The buyer app shows the cancelled order's status, reason and refund chips like a rejected one's, with no pickup details.
- Built (#16, ADR 0009): what an order edit takes off an order and isn't refunded is owed to its buyer, derived from the order's summary less what Oja's team settled by hand (`src/lib/owed-refunds.ts`); a cancelled order (#17) owes all that is left of its share the same way. Each refund Paystack refuses is a `failed` order refund with Paystack's reason, an attempt; a failure after Paystack made the refund isn't one. The `retry-owed-refunds` job (every 5 minutes, under the order's refund lock, skipping an order with a refund still in Paystack's hands) retries it 5 minutes, an hour, then 6 hours after each refusal. Once the third retry is refused, or for less than Paystack's ₦50 minimum, it is handed to Oja's team once: a notice in the admin's notifications, and the buyer pushed and emailed that the team is sending it. The admin's Owed refunds page (`GET /admin/owed-refunds`) lists each with its attempts and last error; staff retry one now (`POST /admin/owed-refunds/:id/retry`, refused while a refund is in Paystack's hands) or mark it settled by hand (`POST /admin/owed-refunds/:id/settle`, bank transfer or other, with a note), which stops its retries and still takes it off the shop's payout; a later edit owing more starts a fresh backoff. The buyer's order shows "Refund of ₦X pending", or "Our team is sending your ₦X refund" (`owed_refund`).
- Built (#13, ADR 0009): **a refund that needs attention is retried to the buyer's bank account.** Paystack moves a refund to `needs-attention` when it couldn't return the money to the card. Its `refund.*` webhook records when (`needs_attention_at`) and emits `order_refund.needs_attention`; the buyer is pushed and emailed a link to the refund's screen in the app (`/refund/:id`; the email links `oja://refund/:id`), and their order shows "Add a bank account" (`refund_needing_account`). A refund of no order has no buyer to ask, so the admin is notified to retry it from Paystack's dashboard. On the screen the buyer picks a bank from Paystack's list (`GET /store/refunds/:id/banks`) and enters a 10-digit account number; Paystack resolves it to its holder's name (`POST /store/refunds/:id/resolve-account`), the buyer confirms, and `POST /store/refunds/:id/retry` sends `POST /refund/retry_with_customer_details/:id` (the bank's Paystack id from the list, so no second lookup), under the order's refund lock. Anyone's account will do: the name isn't matched. Paystack queues it again (`processing`) and its webhooks carry on as for any refund. The account number goes to Paystack only: both routes are POSTs (no number in a URL or access log), it is never stored or logged (a refused Paystack request's error leaves out the query), Sentry isn't sent those request bodies and blanks any `account_number` in its events (`src/lib/bank-account-redaction.ts`), and the refund keeps the bank's name and the last 4 digits (`account_bank_name`, `account_last4`) for support. With no answer, the hourly `follow-up-refund-accounts` job reminds the buyer by push and email after 1 and 3 days, once each, and after 7 hands the refund to Oja's team (`handed_over_at`): it still counts as refunded on the order (Paystack holds the money) and stops holding up the payout, and the order owes it by hand under Owed refunds (`by_hand`; `last_error` "The buyer gave no bank account in 7 days" while nothing else is owed), while Paystack still tries the rest of what the order owes, which notifies the admin and tells the buyer the team is sending it. The team asks the buyer for an account and retries it from Paystack's dashboard (its webhook then settles it, and it is owed no more), or sends it by hand and marks it settled. The buyer can no longer add an account once it is handed over (409).
- **Courier label** (#24): a packed order's screen (and the Packed confirmation) books its Shipbubble label; the order then shows the courier, the waybill and a tracking link, and says when the courier has confirmed. When Shipbubble cancels the shipment the order is back to Packed and offers "Rebook courier label". When the courier booking would pick costs more than the top-up cap above what the buyer paid (#19), nothing is booked and the screen lists the couriers quoting now, cheapest first, with each one's price against the buyer's and whether it needs Oja's approval; staff choose one ("Book X", or "Ask Oja to approve X" above the cap). While a courier waits for approval the order says which and at what price. A booked label with another courier than the buyer's says "instead of" theirs.
- **Handover and tracking** (#25): once the courier confirms, staff tap "Handed to courier" (`POST /vendor/orders/:id/hand-over`, `confirmHandoverWorkflow`), recorded with the member and time. The order is Handed to courier only when Shipbubble's `picked_up` has arrived too, in either order; until then the order screen says which half is missing. Shipbubble's `in_transit` and `completed` then move it to In transit and Delivered. A transit or delivery Shipbubble reports before the handover is complete counts as its pickup and is remembered, so the staff tap takes the order straight to where the courier says it is; a missed `in_transit` doesn't hold up delivery, and a pickup (or later status) arriving before `confirmed` confirms the courier and counts. The handover step's time on the timeline is the courier's pickup. Delivered sets the order fulfilment row's `payout_eligible_at` (what #29's payouts look for), marks the order's Medusa fulfilment delivered and emits `order_fulfilment.delivered`. When one half has waited two hours for the other (`handoverDisagreement`, `@oja/domain`), the `alert-handover-disagreements` job (every 15 minutes) pushes the shop's owners (`seller_member.is_owner`), and again each day it lasts (`handoverAlertDue`; `handover_alerted_at` is the last alert, cleared when a shipment is cancelled), so a delivered order can't wait unpaid on a missed push; any member, the owner too, can confirm the handover. Nothing moves an order cancelled meanwhile: courier updates are acknowledged and ignored, the staff tap is refused, and it never becomes payout-eligible. Each transition records when the order reached its state (`reached`, `recordReached`); vendor order responses carry the staff `timeline` and `handover` (who, by name, and when; when the courier picked it up), and the buyer's order history carries the buyer's `timeline` (`fulfilmentTimeline`) and the shipment's tracking link, without who handed it over. Both apps show the timeline (`Timeline`, `@oja/ui`); the buyer's order shows "Track your parcel".
- Push notifications (Expo): new order, accept deadline approaching, courier pickup scheduled, pickup order not collected.
  - #27 (ADR 0008): each device registers its Expo push token for the person signed in on it (`POST /store/push-tokens`, `POST /vendor/push-tokens`; `DELETE …/:token` at sign-out). A token belongs to one person: registering it again moves it. Tokens live in `src/modules/push-token`; pushes go out on the notification module's `push` channel through our Expo provider (`src/modules/expo-push`, `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN` optional). Every device of every member of the order's shop is pushed on `order.placed`, `order_fulfilment.accept_deadline_near` (once, when two hours are left; the `remind-accept-deadlines` job, marked by `accept_reminded_at`) and `order_fulfilment.pickup_scheduled` (emitted when the courier confirms a booked label, #24). Each device gets each event once (the notification's idempotency key). A push carries `url`, a path in the receiving app (`/orders/:id` in the vendor app; the buyer app's order screen is `/order/:group_id`, for #28), and a vendor push `seller_id`; tapping it opens that screen, acting for that shop (`pushTarget`, `@oja/domain`). A device Expo reports as `DeviceNotRegistered`, on the push's ticket or on its receipt (the hourly `check-push-receipts` job), is forgotten.
- **Pause / resume orders** (owner/manager only): closes the shop now, until reopened or until a set time (see Shop hours and closures).
- Pickup counter: scan the buyer's QR or type the 6-digit code, record who collected.

### Order state machine

Each seller order's **fulfilment method** (delivery or pickup) is fixed when the order is created. Both share the start; each then has its own typed path, so a pickup order can never book a label and a delivery order can never take a pickup code. The courier provider is one implementation of the delivery path (own riders would be another).

```
New → Accepted → Picking → Packed ─┬─ delivery: Label booked → Courier confirmed
                                   │      → Handed to courier → In transit → Delivered
                                   └─ pickup:   Ready for pickup → Collected
```

| State | Source |
|---|---|
| New … Packed | Ours (picking = line-item checklist, optional barcode scan) |
| Label booked | Shipbubble `shipment.label.created` / `pending` |
| Courier confirmed | Shipbubble `confirmed` |
| Handed to courier | Staff tap (who/when) **and** Shipbubble `picked_up`; flag if they disagree for hours |
| In transit | Shipbubble `in_transit` |
| Delivered | Shipbubble `completed` → payout eligible |

| Ready for pickup | Staff tap Packed on a pickup order (one tap); pickup code issued, buyer notified |
| Collected | Staff verify the pickup code → payout eligible |

Branches: **Rejected** (seller declines → refund); **Cancelled** (an accepted order the shop's owners or managers, or Oja's operators, cancel with a reason before a label is booked → full refund of its share, stock returned, #17); **Partially available** (order edit + partial refund); **Shipment cancelled** (Shipbubble `cancelled` → back to Packed, rebook); **Not collected** (pickup hold expires → refund, stock returned, see Pickup). Delivery is Shipbubble-only in v1.

### Shop hours and closures (ADR 0004)

- Every shop has **weekly opening hours** (per weekday, several periods or closed); an onboarding gate. Hours are in the shop's **region's timezone** (`Africa/Lagos` for NG).
- **Date exceptions** (one date or a range): closed (optional note) or special hours, which is also how a shop opens on a normally closed day. **Pause orders now** (vendor app) closes the shop at once, until reopened or a set time.
- Weekly hours and planned exceptions are managed in the web vendor panel; pause/resume in the vendor app. Owner/manager only.
- Our hours module is the **only source of truth**. Mercur's `closed_from` / `closed_to` / `closure_note` are unused and writes to them are refused.
- **Outside regular hours** the shop is orderable: it shows "Closed · opens 8am Mon", checkout says the order is prepared when the shop opens, and delivery estimates count from the next opening.
- **During an exception or pause** the shop is visible but not orderable: "Closed until Tue 14 Oct" with its note; items stay on the shop page and in wishlists but can't be added to the cart, and checkout refuses its cart lines.
- **Open-hours clocks**: the accept deadline (24h, #20) and the pickup hold count only time the shop is open. A pause stops new orders but not the accept clock of orders already waiting, which are still accepted or auto-rejected at their original deadline (#18); it does freeze the pickup hold. Existing accepted orders carry on, and the app warns when pausing that waiting New orders must still be accepted or they'll be rejected at their deadline.
- Built (#68): `src/modules/shop-hours` (weekly hours, exceptions, pauses; a resumed pause is kept, ended, so the clocks still know it), `src/lib/shop-hours.ts`, and the pure `isOpenAt`, `nextOpening`, `openTimeBetween`, `afterOpenTime` and `shopStatus` in `@oja/domain`. The timezone is the region's `metadata.time_zone`, found from the shop's pickup address country. Listings, shop pages and cart and checkout groups carry `shop_status`: `open`, `closed` (outside hours, orderable, `opens_at`) or `closed_until` (exception or pause, not orderable, `until` = next opening, `note`), with a `label`. Adding a `closed_until` shop's offer to a cart and starting payment are refused (400); changing a quantity already in the cart isn't. Vendor routes: `GET /vendor/shop-hours` (any member; includes `new_orders` for the pause warning), `POST /vendor/shop-hours/weekly`, `POST|DELETE /vendor/shop-hours/exceptions[/:id]`, `POST /vendor/shop-hours/pause` and `/resume` (seller update: owners and managers). Writes carrying `closed_from`, `closed_to` or `closure_note` to `POST /vendor/sellers/me|:id` and `POST /admin/sellers[/:id]` are refused (400). Live shops are Mercur sellers with status `open` past every gate; Mercur's closure no longer counts. The pickup hold uses the same clock once pickup is built. Delivery estimates (checkout, the product page, and the buyer's order, which shows the one saved at checkout) count from the shop's next opening (#20, `deliveryStartsAt` and `etaCountedFrom` in `@oja/domain`, `quoteRates` in `src/lib/delivery.ts`): rates are asked for with `pickup_date` on the opening's day, and a courier whose `pickup_eta_time` comes before the opening has its ETA moved later by the gap (ADR 0004); the buyer's order also says it is prepared when the shop opens while it is New and the shop closed. Pickup orders get no "ready by" estimate.

### Pickup

- Split into #12 (the pickup path, built: see below), #32 (hold, Not collected, built: see below) and #33 (notifications, built: see below); decisions grilled on 2026-10-11.
- A shop opts in by setting one **collection point** (owners and managers; only once the shop has opening hours): by default it *follows* the shop's pickup address (ticking it consents to showing that address to buyers); unticked, it is a separate address entered as in ADR 0002, with a map pin and no Shipbubble check. Always with buyer-facing instructions (and optional phone). It uses the shop's hours. Pickup is free.
- At checkout delivery stays preselected; a perishable shop no courier reaches in time switches to pickup where it has a collection point (the buyer can still remove the fresh items instead). An all-pickup checkout needs no delivery address. Checkout states the hold and the perishable rule.
- The **pickup code** is per seller order, random, issued at Ready for pickup and shown only in the buyer's order and their Ready for pickup email (#33): 6 digits plus a QR of them. Server-verified; 5 wrong tries per order in 15 minutes, then refused until the window passes. Whoever holds the code may collect (bearer); any member who works on orders can hand over, sees the buyer's name and items, and must record who collected. Collected makes the order payout-eligible at once (no delivery fee in the payout).
- Collection is **all or nothing**, accepted only in Ready for pickup, allowed outside hours if staff are present. No switching method after payment.
- Built (#12): a fulfilment carries its `method`, fixed when the order is placed from its shipping method (`@oja/domain` types refuse courier events on pickup orders and code events on delivery orders). Collection points live in `src/modules/collection-point` (`GET|POST /vendor/collection-point`, owners and managers set it; refused without weekly hours); setting one adds the shop's free `manual_manual` pickup shipping option, which is how pickup reaches the cart and the order. `POST /store/carts/:id/checkout` takes `pickup` (seller ids) and either `shipping_address` or, when every shop is collected, `contact` (name, phone); a perishable shop no courier serves in time is switched to pickup when it has a collection point (`pickup_note`). Mark packed on a pickup order lands in Ready for pickup, issues a random 6-digit `pickup_code` (shown only in the buyer's order history, with a QR code in the app); its `order_fulfilment.state_changed` pushes the buyer (`buyerStatusPush`'s `ready_for_pickup`: the code is in the app, the address and the shop's hours). The other pickup notifications are #33 (below). `POST /vendor/orders/:id/collect` (`{ code, collected_by }`) checks the code (`checkPickupCode`: five wrong in 15 minutes refuse every code, 429) and records the member, time and collector; Collected sets `payout_eligible_at`, and the payout has no delivery fee. `DELETE /vendor/collection-point` stops a shop offering pickup (its pickup option goes too), refused while one of its pickup orders is still to collect. `GET /vendor/ready-for-pickup` lists a shop's Ready orders however old, for the vendor app's list. The seed gives Adire House a collection point.
- **Hold**: 5 open days after Ready (an open day is a calendar day the shop opens; the Ready day and closed days don't count), ending at closing time; perishable orders until the shop closes on its next open day after Ready (mixed orders take the shorter). The clock freezes while the shop is paused with no resume date. Reminders at half-way and on the last day. On expiry → **Not collected**, which is final: non-perishable lines are removed by order edit and refunded, their stock returned; **perishable lines are not refunded** and are paid out to the shop less commission (stated at checkout).
- Built (#32): `collectBy` (`@oja/domain`, on `afterOpenDays` in `shop-hours.ts`) counts the hold from when the order reached Ready for pickup, in the shop's hours; the order's lines still on it, perishable or not (#69), set its length. Vendor and buyer order responses carry `fulfilment.collect_by` (`{ at, time_zone }`, null outside Ready for pickup and while the clock is frozen), which the buyer app shows under the code and the vendor app on its ready-for-pickup list and order page (`collectByLabel`: "Collect by 6pm, Thu 15 Oct"). The `expire-pickup-holds` job (every 5 minutes) runs `expirePickupHoldWorkflow` on each order past its hold: its non-perishable lines are set to none by the same Mercur order edit as items removed while picking (#22), so the total, commission and stock reservations follow (the lines leave the order), and the order moves to `not_collected` (system trigger `pickup_hold_expired`; no code is taken there). What the edit took off the total is then refunded (`refundOwedWorkflow`; a refund Paystack turns down is retried, as for #22). Perishable lines keep their reservations; while any remain the order gets `payout_eligible_at`, so `payoutOf` pays the shop what was captured less the refund, less 10%. An order with no perishable lines is refunded in full and never paid out. Reminders are #33.
- Built (#33): each of these is an email paired with a push, once per order (and per device), keyed like the other buyer notifications (ADR 0008). **Ready for pickup**: the push (#12) and an email with the code, the collection point, its instructions and phone, the shop's hours, when to collect by and the order's lines (`tellReadyForPickup`). **Reminders**: the `remind-pickup-holds` job (every 5 minutes) reminds the buyer of each order still Ready for pickup half-way through its hold (from its middle open day, the 3rd of 5) and on its last day, only while the shop is open (`holdReminderDue`, `@oja/domain`), saying when to collect by and that it is refunded otherwise (except fresh items). `remindPickupHoldWorkflow` marks each once (`hold_reminded`), under the order's lock and only while it is still Ready for pickup, so a collected order is never reminded; a half-way reminder not sent by the last day is skipped, and a one-day (perishable) hold has only the last day's. **Collected**: `order_fulfilment.collected` emails the receipt (its lines, what was paid and refunded, who collected it and when) with a push. **Not collected**: `expirePickupHoldWorkflow` emits `order_fulfilment.not_collected` (`{ id, amount, refunded, kept, perishable_kept }`); the buyer is told what they are refunded, naming the lines, and which fresh lines aren't; that stands for the refund's own "completed" push, which isn't sent for that refund (the one the expiry made, noted `NOT_COLLECTED_NOTE`); a later refund of the order, such as Oja's team refunding the fresh lines, is pushed as usual. The shop's **owners and managers** (the administration role) are pushed that the order wasn't collected, to put its items back on the shelf, the first shop push for that audience (`pushToShop`'s `to`). Emails of a pickup order show no delivery row.

### Perishables

- Operator-set **perishable** flag on catalogue categories, inherited by products, operator-overridable per product; shops can't change it.
- A seller order with perishable lines is offered only Shipbubble rates whose `delivery_eta_time` is by end of the next day; if none, delivery is unavailable for that shop and pickup is offered where the shop has it.
- Food in v1 means groceries (packaged, fresh, chilled). Cooked food is out of scope.
- Built in #69 (`src/modules/perishable`, `src/lib/perishable.ts`): the flags live in our own module, not on metadata, which shops can write through the vendor product routes. A category's flag also covers its sub-categories; a product is perishable when any of its categories is, unless the operator's override says otherwise. Admin routes only: `GET|POST /admin/product-categories/:id/perishable` (`{ perishable }`) and `GET|POST /admin/products/:id/perishable` (`{ override: true | false | null }`), with widgets on the admin category and product pages. The seed marks Fresh Produce and Chilled & Frozen perishable.
- "End of the next day" is counted in the region's timezone (`metadata.time_zone`, `Africa/Lagos` for NG; `arrivesByEndOfNextDay`, `@oja/domain`) from when checkout quotes. Rates are filtered before best value, cheapest and fastest are picked. A shop with no rate in time gets `delivery_unavailable` (`perishable_too_slow`, with a reason) on its checkout group instead of a delivery; the cart keeps it in `metadata.delivery_unavailable` against what was quoted, any earlier delivery for that shop is dropped, and payment is refused with the reason. Reading the checkout checks again: a perishable shop's delivery that no longer arrives by the end of tomorrow (time passed, or the order became perishable after it was quoted) stops counting, so it is quoted again, and a refusal stands only while the order is perishable. A shop no courier serves at all still refuses the whole checkout, as before. The product page's estimate uses the same filter. Listings' product detail and cart lines carry `perishable`.

## Backend additions (`apps/api`)

1. **Paystack payments** — our own provider (`src/modules/paystack`, ADR 0003). Captured only on webhook confirmation; vendors can't capture. Mercur 2.3.5 has no capture-on-fulfilment job to disable.
2. **Accept/reject module** — `POST /vendor/orders/:id/accept|reject`; scheduled job auto-rejects + refunds orders not accepted within **24 open hours** (ADR 0004, #68: `acceptDeadline` in `@oja/domain` counts the shop's open hours, and a pause starting after the order was placed as open, #18; a shop without hours, which can't be live, falls back to 24 hours). Each order's state lives in `src/modules/order-fulfilment`; vendor order responses carry it as `fulfilment`. Rejecting cancels the order and refunds its share; a refund Paystack turns down is retried by the job.
3. **Paystack payout provider** (`IPayoutProvider`) — Paystack Transfers after delivery. Flat **10%** commission; platform absorbs Paystack fees. Payout amounts computed explicitly: seller promotions borne by seller, platform promotions borne by marketplace. Built (#29, ADR 0009): `src/modules/paystack-payout` on Mercur's payout module, one Paystack account per currency, paying the recipient of the shop's bank account (#26). The daily `pay-out-delivered-orders` job pays each order whose `payout_eligible_at` is set, once (`shop_payout`, one row per attempt, transfer reference `payout_<order id>`), unless it has a refund Paystack hasn't settled: what was captured for it, less its refunds of items and what it still owes the buyer for lines an order edit removed, less the delivery fee (Oja pays the courier), plus the discounts Oja funds on what the buyer kept (ADR 0010: recorded per order when placed), less 10% of the rest (`payoutOf`, `src/lib/payouts.ts`). A refund after a payout is taken from the shop's next payouts (`owedBack`). Paystack's `transfer.*` webhooks arrive with its payment webhooks and, checked with Paystack, mark the payout paid or failed; a queued transfer is `pending` (Mercur's payout table has no `processing`). The daily `check-paystack-refunds` job flags, in the log and the admin's notifications, any Paystack refund of the day before that no order refund records. The seed sets Mercur's global commission to 10% so each order's commission shows in the vendor panel; its order page also shows the order's payout (`GET /vendor/orders/:id/payout`, `payout:read`), and Mercur's Payouts page lists them. A failed transfer is tried again on the next run, up to three attempts, then the admin is told to pay it by hand.
4. **Shipbubble fulfillment provider** — address validation, per-seller calculated rates, label on fulfilment, cancel, HMAC-SHA512-verified webhooks → state updates. Labels paid from the platform's Shipbubble wallet. Rates (#23, `src/lib/delivery.ts`): checkout validates the buyer's address (pin first) and keeps its `shipbubble_address_code`; each shop's pickup address is validated when it is saved (onboarding gates, #26), or when first quoted if it was saved before. Each shop has one calculated shipping option (`shipbubble_shipbubble`); checkout fetches the shop's rates (grams → kg, one parcel per shop) and stores the chosen rate, its choices and what they were quoted for on the shipping method's data, which is the method's price. A cart changed since is quoted again before payment. The store's own `POST /store/carts/:id/shipping-methods` is closed; `POST /store/carts/:id/checkout/delivery` picks another quoted choice. Best value is the cheapest rate arriving within a day of the fastest (`deliveryChoices`, `@oja/domain`). `SHIPBUBBLE_API_KEY` is required outside tests. Labels (#24): staff book one for a packed order (`POST /vendor/orders/:id/book-label`, `bookLabelWorkflow`). Checkout's request token can't be booked later, so the parcel is quoted again and the buyer's courier booked if it still quotes; otherwise the cheapest one arriving no later, otherwise the fastest (`rateToBook`). The platform pays what the booked courier costs above the buyer's checkout price only up to the **top-up cap** (#19, decisions grilled 2026-10-17): a flat amount per shipment, ₦1,000 until Oja's team change it in the admin (Orders › Courier approvals, kept in the store's `metadata.courier_top_up_cap`, `src/lib/top-up-cap.ts`; `GET /admin/courier-approvals`, `POST /admin/courier-approvals/cap`). Within it the label is booked; above it `POST /vendor/orders/:id/book-label` books nothing and answers `courier_options` (every courier quoting now, its `difference` and `within_cap`, `decideLabel`), and staff choose one by sending `{ courier: { courier_id, service_code } }`; a choice is refused while booking would book a courier within the cap by itself. A chosen courier within the cap is booked; one above it waits on the order fulfilment row's `courier_approval` (shown to staff as `fulfilment.courier_approval`) and Oja's team are notified in the admin's notifications. `POST /admin/courier-approvals/:id/approve` quotes again and books it at its price now, by the operator (refused if it no longer quotes, or now costs more than when staff asked: the operator declines it); `…/decline` clears it, for the shop to choose again. The buyer is never charged more, and the shop never pays the difference. Each shipment records the courier booked and its price, the buyer's courier at checkout and its price, the difference, and the booked courier's ETA (`Shipment`); staff see them as `shipment.switched_from` and `shipment.price`. The label is booked on a Medusa fulfilment of the order's lines through the provider's `createFulfillment`, paid from the platform's wallet; the provider books only a rate the workflow passes in the fulfilment's `metadata.shipbubble_rate`. The shipment id (`SB-…`) is the waybill, kept with the tracking link as the fulfilment's label and as the order fulfilment row's `shipment`. Shipbubble's webhooks arrive at `POST /hooks/shipbubble`, checked against `x-ship-signature` (HMAC-SHA512 of the raw body keyed with the API key; 401 otherwise). They move only the order whose current shipment they name: `pending` → Label booked, `confirmed` → Courier confirmed (emits `order_fulfilment.pickup_scheduled`), `cancelled` → Packed, with the shipment kept as cancelled and its fulfilment cancelled without asking Shipbubble again; staff then rebook. `picked_up` is the courier's half of the handover, `in_transit` → In transit, `completed` → Delivered (#25). A status the order's state doesn't take now (a repeat, a retry about a rebooked shipment) is acknowledged and ignored. Mercur's own vendor routes to create or cancel a fulfilment are closed, so the state can't be bypassed.
5. **Vendor onboarding gates** (ADR 0007) — Shipbubble-validated pickup address (Google autocomplete + map pin, ADR 0002) + Paystack-resolved bank account (transfer recipient) + weekly opening hours; offers hidden until all are complete. Web vendor panel extensions. Built (#26, `src/lib/onboarding.ts`): a shop is **live** when Mercur has it open and it has passed every gate. The pickup address passes once it carries the pin its owner confirmed on the map and a `shipbubble_address_code`, which only a validated save writes (#41: no route saves a pickup address without a pin): the checklist's `POST /vendor/onboarding/pickup-address` checks the country's form (`@oja/domain`) and Mercur's own vendor and admin address routes are validated first too, so a refused address is never saved and the code always belongs to the address it sits on. The bank account passes once Paystack has resolved it and made a transfer recipient (`POST /vendor/onboarding/bank-account`, banks from `GET /vendor/onboarding/banks`), kept in `src/modules/shop-bank-account` rather than Mercur's payment details, which members can write unchecked. Our listing, shop, wishlist and cart routes read `liveSellerIds`; Mercur's `/store/offers`, `/store/sellers` and `/store/products` see only live shops' rows (`api/store/live-shops.ts`). Owners and managers enter the details; staff see the checklist (`GET /vendor/onboarding`). A buyer's own order groups and reviews still show a shop that has stopped being live. The vendor panel's Shop setup page lists what is missing, where the pickup address is found with Google (`/vendor/address-lookup/*`, in the region of the shop's currency) and pinned on a Google Maps JavaScript map (#41), and a banner on Orders and Offers until the shop is live. The seed takes its shops through the same gates (placeholders only where Shipbubble or Paystack isn't configured, as in tests). Weekly hours are the third gate (#68): set on the vendor panel's Opening hours page.
6. **Shipping attributes** — weight/dimensions/package category defaults per product category, vendor-overridable; weight required. Perishable flag per category, operator-only.
7. **Clerk auth provider** — custom Medusa auth provider verifying Clerk JWT, mapping to customer auth identity.
8. **Public review routes** — `GET /store/products/:id/reviews`, `GET /store/sellers/:id/reviews`: published reviews only, with their average rating and count. Built (#7, `src/lib/reviews.ts`) on Mercur's review module (`@mercurjs/core`: one `review` per target, a shop or a catalogue product, linked to its order and buyer, `pending` → `published` / `rejected`).
   - **Only the seller route is gated on live shops** (ADR 0007): it is not found for a shop that isn't live. The product route deliberately isn't: a product review is of the shared catalogue product, whichever shop sold it, and names no shop, so it stays true of the product when that shop stops trading.
   - Both list newest first (`limit`, `offset`); the database averages and counts the published reviews. Reviewers show by first name and initial (`reviewerName`).
   - **One review per buyer of each shop and product**, editable, not one per order. Oja sells everyday goods that buyers order again and again; a review per order would weight a shop's or product's rating toward its most frequent buyers and ask them to review the same rice every week. `POST /store/reviews` replaces Mercur's handler with `submitReviewWorkflow`: the order must be the buyer's, have reached its terminal success state (`canReview`, `@oja/domain`: Delivered, or Collected for an in-store pickup) and have brought them the shop or a product they kept, and the buyer must not have reviewed that shop or product already, on any order (400, pointing them to edit). `POST /store/reviews/:id` (the author's edit) sends the review back to `pending`.
   - The buyer's order history carries each order's `can_review` and the buyer's `reviews` of its shop and products, whichever order each was written on, with their status. A received order offers "Review this order", or "Edit your review" once all are written (`reviewAction`, `reviewForm`). The shop's reply shows only once a review is published, on the public routes and the buyer's own (`GET /store/reviews*`, which replace Mercur's).
   - **Moderation** is the operator's, on Mercur's admin panel Reviews page (status `published` / `rejected`, `POST /admin/reviews/:id`); **replies** are the shop's, on Mercur's vendor panel Reviews page (`POST /vendor/reviews/:id`, one reply per review). Mercur's vendor routes reach only reviews of the shop itself, so product reviews get no shop reply.
   - **My reviews** (designed 2026-10-21, not built): an Account screen listing every review the buyer wrote, across shops and products, newest first, with its moderation status, the shop's reply once published, and edit (back to moderation) or delete. It needs no backend change: it reads `GET /store/reviews`, the buyer's own reviews, and edits or deletes with `POST`/`DELETE /store/reviews/:id`. The buyer app needs a list hook, a `my-reviews` route and an Account row.
9. **Wishlist** (#32) — Mercur's `wishlist` block (products only), adapted: products are always one shop's offer, so each saved product keeps the shop it was saved from (a `seller_id` column on the wishlist–product link; saving it from another shop replaces it). `GET /store/wishlist` answers each as that shop's listing in the region (null once the shop stops selling it there), and `POST /store/wishlist/product/:id/cart` moves it into the buyer's own cart as that shop's offer, asking which variant when there are several. Signed-in only; the app sends a guest to sign in.
10. **Notifications** — Resend provider with React Email templates (order confirmation, delivery receipt); every email paired with an Expo push; push for all status changes. Built (#5, `src/lib/buyer-notifications.tsx`): emails go out on the notification module's `email` channel through our Resend provider (`src/modules/resend`; the caller renders the React Email template in `src/emails`, the provider sends its subject, HTML and text from `RESEND_FROM`), or are only logged by the local provider while `RESEND_API_KEY` is unset. Mercur's `order_group.created`, emitted once Paystack's webhook has captured the payment, sends the buyer the confirmation (each shop's order with its lines, delivery and what was paid, the total and the delivery address) and pushes it to each of their devices; a shop closed at checkout outside its hours says it prepares the order when it opens, and when (`openingLabel`). Every fulfilment transition that changes an order's state emits `order_fulfilment.state_changed` (`{ id, state }`); the buyer is pushed for the states `buyerStatusPush` (`@oja/domain`) lists: accepted, label booked, picked up by the courier, delivered, and rejected (by the shop or the accept deadline, with the refund). A label booked with another courier than the buyer chose (#19) is told by push and email instead ("New courier for order #N": the courier now shipping it and when it arrives, `courierChangedPush`, `CourierChangedEmail`), on `order_fulfilment.label_booked`, once per booking; the buyer's order then shows the booked courier and its ETA, with the courier they chose (`delivery.switched_from`, `courierChangedNote`). `order_fulfilment.delivered` emails the receipt for that shop's order (its refund too, if any). Removing items a shop didn't have (#22) emits `order_fulfilment.items_removed` (`{ id, amount, removed, at }`) once the edit succeeds, and the buyer is pushed which shop removed what (two lines named, more counted) and what they are refunded. When Paystack's `refund.*` webhook moves an order's refund to processed, `syncOrderRefundsWorkflow` emits `order_refund.processed` (`{ id }`, the order refund) and the buyer is pushed that the refund of its amount from the shop has completed: once, since a settled refund isn't fetched again and the push is keyed by the refund; nothing for a refund still pending or failed (those are retried). A refund that moves to needing attention emits `order_refund.needs_attention` (`{ id }`), and the buyer is pushed and emailed to add a bank account for it, then reminded after 1 and 3 days (#13). The messages are in `@oja/domain` (`buyer-push.ts`). A buyer push carries `url` `/order/:group_id`, which the app opens when tapped, and `order_group_id` (and `order_id` for a status). Each email is sent once per checkout or order, and each push once per device each time the order reaches its state, so a rebooked courier is told again (idempotency keys); an email that can't be sent (no address, or Resend refuses it) is logged and the push still goes.
11. **A shop sees only its own order** (#61, #62; `src/lib/shop-order.ts`, applied to every `/vendor/orders*` and `/vendor/payments*` response). A checkout split across shops has one Paystack payment, which Mercur's vendor order reads put on every order, and a line's variant carries every shop's price for it (offers share the variant's price set). Each order instead shows its share of the payment: captured and refunded from the order's own transactions, the other amounts scaled to that share, with Paystack's record of the whole charge and the payment sessions left out. A refund is likewise only the order's share, at most its own captures less its own refunds, and is recorded against the order (#20, `refundOrderShareWorkflow`): a `refund` order transaction whose `reference_id` is Medusa's refund, and an order refund with Paystack's refund id and status, which Paystack's `refund.*` webhooks update. A failed refund is taken off the order and refunded again. The web vendor panel's refund route goes through it too. A line's variant carries only the line's own price (its compare-at price when it was on sale, else what the buyer paid), because the web vendor panel strikes the variant's price through beside the line's: dropping the prices would strike through ₦0, and the shop's current offer price would strike through after any price change.
   - **The admin shows both** (#14, `src/lib/admin-checkout.ts`). Every `GET /admin/orders*` and `/admin/order-groups*` response gives each order its own `payment_status`, worked out from its own share as above (`ownPaymentStatus`), so in a checkout where one shop rejected its order, that order reads Refunded and the other Captured where Mercur had both "partially refunded". Its `payment_collections` stay the checkout's one payment, as Paystack has it; `fulfillment_status` is already each order's own. The admin's order page has a Checkout widget (`GET /admin/orders/:id/checkout`: `{ checkout: { id, display_id, currency_code, orders, payment } }`) listing every order of the group with its shop, own payment and fulfilment status and what was captured and refunded of it, and the shared payment's status, amounts and Paystack reference. The order list's group rows stay blank: Mercur's admin panel sets their Payment and Fulfillment cells to null whatever the API sends, a leftover for the panels' go-live pass (#21).
12. **A shop's price edit touches only its own prices** (#84; `src/workflows/offer-prices`). Offers of a variant share its price set, told apart by an `offer_id` price rule. Mercur 2.3.5's `updateOffersWorkflow` writes an offer's prices as the set's whole list, which deletes every other shop's price for the variant. Our `POST /vendor/offers/:id` replaces Mercur's handler (Mercur's validation, RBAC policy, GET and DELETE still apply) and, like the offer import's re-price, sets only the offer's own prices: changed and added through the pricing module, the dropped ones unlinked and removed, all undone if the rest of the update fails. The operator's product routes can't write a variant's prices either (#15; `src/api/admin/products/no-variant-prices.ts`): a body carrying variant `prices` on product or variant create, update or batch is refused (400), as is a `POST /admin/products/imports` sheet with a value in a "Variant Price …" column, and Medusa's deprecated upload import is refused outright since its sheet can't be checked. Even on create, a price without an `offer_id` rule would fill an offer's gap in its currency. Prices stay with offers and price lists.

## Catalogue & product import

### What Mercur provides (v2.3.5)

- **Shared, operator-owned catalogue.** Products/variants hold content (title, description, images, weight, dimensions, barcode/EAN/UPC, attributes). Each seller's **offer** holds price, stock, seller SKU, lead time, backorder, shipping profile.
- Sellers can **propose** new products (`proposed` → admin confirm / reject / request changes). Later edits go through the `product-edit` change-request pipeline (low-risk edits auto-confirm).
- **Product attributes module**: global, category-linked typed attributes (`single_select`, `multi_select`, `unit`, `toggle`, `text`). `is_required` is only enforced client-side in Mercur; Oja's publish gate enforces required category attributes server-side (#33). Store filtering works only for variant-axis attributes.
- **Import:** admin CSV import UI existed in v2.0–v2.1 and was removed in v2.2.0 (2026-07-03, likely because the offer model moved prices off variants). Medusa's `/admin/products/import` API still exists. Vendor import exists only as the optional `product-import-export` registry block, which creates **draft products, not offers**, ignores the seller, and expects Medusa's full CSV format.
- No barcode lookup or enrichment.

### What we add

**A. Platform catalogue import + enrichment (admin).** Based on the removed v2.1 admin import page. Upload CSV → column mapping (saved per source) → preview with row errors → create via Mercur product workflows. Each created product is queued for **enrichment in the background** (worker), which fills its empty fields and then publishes it or sends it for review (see Rules). The import answers straight away; enrichment runs at each source's rate limit.

**B. Vendor offer import (vendor panel).** Adapt the `product-import-export` block. Vendor uploads any list (barcode, name, selling price, discount price, cost, stock…). Rows whose barcode matches a catalogue variant → **offers**. Unmatched rows → **seller-linked proposals** carrying whatever fields the vendor had; they land in the admin queue where A's enrichment runs before approval.

**C. Vendor app quick-add** (#38, ADR 0006). Scan (or type) a barcode → `GET /vendor/quick-add/:barcode` says what it is to the shop. A catalogue product the shop doesn't sell: set price and stock → `POST /vendor/quick-add` creates the offer. Unmatched: take a photo + enter name/price → the same route proposes the product with that photo (a vendor proposal, enriched and reviewed like an import row) and offers it. Open to fulfilment staff through a custom permission check, not a wider role; it only ever adds, so a product the shop already sells is refused. Offers record the member who added them (`created_by`, and `metadata.added_with = "quick_add"`), shown on the web vendor panel's offer page.

### Shop-scoped listings (no price comparison)

- Local shops object to side-by-side price comparison, so v1 never shows several shops' prices for the same product together.
- Search and category results are **grouped by shop**: a shop header (name, area, rating, delivery estimate) with a row of that shop's matching products and "See all". Shops come where their best result comes in the listing order below, so nearest / fastest first among equal matches — never by lowest price. The header's delivery estimate is the shop's usual ship time, the same wherever the shop appears; ratings join with reviews. A product may appear under two shops, but never side by side.
- The product page is always in a shop's context: that shop's price, delivery estimate and rating; then summary, details (attributes), reviews, "more from this shop" and "you might also like" (same shop).
- **Listing order** (every listing route: feed, search, category, shop): best name match first (whole name, then start of the name, then every search word starting a word of the name, then anywhere inside it), then the nearest **distance band** (under 5 km, 5–15 km, over 15 km; `@oja/domain`), then the shop that ships soonest (its offers' slowest lead time, else the shop's default; unset last), then the nearest shop, then the order listings were first made. Never by price. Distance is straight-line, from the pin of the buyer's "Delivering to" saved address (picked at the top of Home and Search, the default to start with, remembered on the device; checkout starts on it) to the shop's pickup pin. The routes take that address as `address_id`, resolved against the signed-in buyer's own addresses; without one (guests, no saved address, someone else's) the order is name match, ship time, first listed. Buyers see only a rounded distance ("under 1 km", then whole km), never a shop's coordinates (ADR 0011, #43). `GET /store/listings` takes `q` (every word must appear in the product name) and `category_id` (the category and those below it); Every listing's seller carries its area, usual ship time and rounded distance (`distance_km`) for shop headers; `GET /store/shops/:seller_id` adds the description for a shop page, again only for live shops serving the region (ADR 0007), open or closed (ADR 0004) (#14).
- Mercur's store API shows the cheapest offer on a product by default; we add shop-scoped store routes (`GET /store/listings`, `GET /store/listings/:seller_id/:product_id`: offer + product + seller, only for live shops serving the region) and don't use the cheapest-offer price in the buyer app. A listing's headline price is that shop's cheapest variant, shown as "From" when its variants differ (`@oja/domain`).
- Buyers can still add products from several shops to one cart; comparison is simply not surfaced.

### Rules

- **Field schema:** importers accept any subset of Medusa product/variant fields plus offer fields; mapping options are generated from the schema (including category attributes). GTIN/ISBN/ASIN map to EAN/UPC (Mercur doesn't store them). Barcodes are always read as text; prices like "₦1,500" parsed.
- **Minimum to create a row:** barcode or title + selling price. **Minimum to publish:** title, ≥1 image, category, weight, required category attributes — enforced **server-side**. Vendors/admin see a completeness score and missing-field list.
- **A selling price is required to list, not to publish** (#35). A master product's price comes from shops' offers, and buyers only ever see a product through an open shop's priced offer in their region, so an unpriced published product is invisible to buyers. Publishing no longer waits for a price because shops can only offer on *published* catalogue products in the vendor panel (Mercur's vendor product filter); a price rule at publish deadlocked every operator import.
- **Enrichment order** (per field, lower sources only fill empty fields, provenance recorded per field): supplied data → existing catalogue (by barcode) → configured product databases in priority order → AI draft (#36). The AI draft is the `ai_draft` source, last in `ENRICHMENT_SOURCES` (included by default when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set): Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`, structured output) sees the product's photos (fetched from where they were copied from, not our storage) and its data so far, and drafts only the fields still empty: title, description, weight or a catalogue category, never images. Each drafted field's provenance is flagged `drafted`, the admin's Enrichment page and the product page's Enrichment widget (where it is approved) mark it "AI draft", and the product always goes to review, even if a deployment lists `ai_draft` as trusted.
- **Sources are pluggable adapters, chosen by config** (`ENRICHMENT_SOURCES`: which run and in what order, the catalogue first by default; keys per adapter), so production can swap databases without code changes. Each adapter declares its licence as a **policy** that the worker enforces: *may show data publicly* (else its fields aren't used for listing content), *image rights* (else its images aren't copied), *attribution* (shown to buyers only when required), and a rate limit. Whether a source is **trusted** (drives approval) is the deployment's call, not the adapter's: `ENRICHMENT_TRUSTED_SOURCES`. **Open Food / Beauty / Products Facts** (ODbL / CC BY-SA, attribution required) is the demo adapter. Production is expected to use curated databases (e.g. Go-UPC) under negotiated terms; each adapter's policy must match its contract (Go-UPC's public ToS forbids making its data public and grants no image rights).
- **Approval is by who vouches for the data.** Operator imports are reviewed by the operator at preview, so they need no second review: once enriched they publish if complete. A product needs human review (Mercur's `proposed` queue) when a field came from an *untrusted* source, or when its submitter is untrusted (vendor proposals, #37) and no trusted source matched with an image and a category. AI drafts always need a human. Offers on existing products publish immediately. Admins can list what enrichment auto-published, and why, to spot-check it.
- **Images:** only stored in R2 (Medusa's file module) with source recorded; never hotlinked, including image URLs supplied in a sheet. Product photos are shown **contained on a white tile with no outline, in both themes; spacing separates tiles; product cards never carry a save heart: saving to the wishlist happens only on the product screen (decided 2026-10-21)** (not full-bleed, never cropped; about a 10% margin). Catalogue pack shots are almost always on white, so they blend into the tile with no processing, cutouts sit on it, and vendor or Open Food Facts photos show whole, framed by it (decided 2026-10-20 in the Adire redesign). Background removal is planned but not built (`enrichment/images.ts` copies images as they are); when it is, the pipeline removes backgrounds (on-device/server segmentation) and the approval queue includes a quick image check (accept cut-out, re-crop, or request a clean pack shot) — segmentation can keep hands holding a product. Quick-add asks staff to photograph products on a flat surface, not in hand.
- **Extra fields** (care instructions, voltage, fabric…) are Mercur category attributes, starting with a small set for grocery, fashion, electronics.
- **Units:** weight in grams, dimensions in cm; importer parses "1.5kg", "30cm"; Shipbubble provider converts to kg.
- **A shop's own discounts are shown; other shops' prices never are.** Shops fear losing buyers to a cheaper competitor, so no screen compares one shop's price with another's. A shop may still discount its own offer and show it: its normal price crossed out beside the sale price (`@oja/domain` `offerPrice`, #13). Sale prices come from sellers' price lists in the vendor panel and are scoped to the seller's own offer, never the shared variant, or one shop's sale would change a competitor's price: the vendor price-list routes refuse a price without an `offer_id` rule naming one of the shop's own offers of that variant (#10, `src/api/vendor/price-lists`). The offer import's optional "Discount (sale) price" field (below the selling price, owners and managers only) sets the same kind of price, always in the shop's "Imported sale prices" list, made on first use: it changes the offer's price there, or adds one, and never touches a sale the shop made itself. A blank cell leaves any sale as it is, and a row can't lower the selling price to or below the offer's imported sale price without lowering that too. A column headed "Sale price" (or "sale_price", "Selling/sale price") is suggested as the selling price, as shops use it for the price they sell at; the discount field is suggested only from unmistakable headers ("Discount price", "Promo price"), and otherwise the shop maps it by hand (#46, `src/lib/offer-import/fields.ts`, `src/workflows/offer-import/set-offer-sale-price.ts`). Cart-level promotions (codes, automatic deals) discount only that shop's items (#6, ADR 0010).
- **Cost price** stored privately on the offer, visible to owner/manager role only; v1 use: below-cost warning. Kept in the `offer-import` module beside the offer (not in its metadata, which fulfilment staff can read), and served by `GET /vendor/offer-costs` behind the `offer:update` RBAC policy, which fulfilment staff lack (#37).
- **Stock** optional; if absent, treat as in stock / not tracked and rely on the pick-time out-of-stock path.

## Multi-country readiness (v1 ships NG only)

Target: independent per-country markets. Cross-border purchasing is out of scope and a separate future project.

1. Everything goes through Medusa regions; no hardcoded `NGN`; apps carry `region_id`, format money via `Intl`.
2. Sellers have a country + currency; offers visible only in regions they can serve.
3. Payment, fulfilment and payout providers selected per region / seller country (Paystack keys per country; Shipbubble serves NG region only; payout provider routes to the right Paystack account internally, since Mercur allows one payout provider).
4. Address forms driven by per-country config (NG: state + LGA); phone formats likewise. Address lookup goes through a per-region `AddressLookupProvider` (Google for NG), restricted to the region's countries server-side (ADR 0002).
5. Order states bound to a generic shipment-provider interface.
