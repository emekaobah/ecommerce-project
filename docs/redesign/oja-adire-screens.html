Numbers and dates

  • Prices are whole naira with ₦ and thousands commas. No kobo.
  • Dates beat durations: "Arrives Thu 23 Oct", never "Ships in 2 days".

Rules that keep it disciplined

  • The adire pattern appears in brand moments only: sign-in, the Home band, shop and order headers, pickup codes, confirmations and the vendor app's headers. It always fades out behind text, so letters never sit on its strokes.
  • Every full-screen surface is indigo or white. Green, ember and red stay at chip, pill and badge size; red also marks destructive buttons.
  • One primary indigo button per screen, carrying the price when money moves.
  • Status chips always pair a symbol and a label with colour.
  • Photos are cut-outs on the theme's tile (white, charcoal in dark), contained and never cropped. Vendors get a photo standard: square, plain background.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do next, in the shopper's words.

Built only on what exists

  • Every screen was checked against the code on 21 Oct 2026. Where a screen needs backend work, its notes say so; the work is in the redesign readiness spec: In progress / Past orders and order search, the shops route for Home, stock in listings, cart and payment, the shop's closing time, logos and banners, receipt totals, refunds that name their items, Order again, vendor stage counts and the new-order alarm.
  • Deals are shown the way the backend has them: a shop's sale price crossed out, and its automatic promotion as a badge. There's no deals feed or countdown, because sale end dates don't reach the buyer.
  • Shops show the logo and banner they upload in the vendor panel, falling back to a monogram and the indigo pattern. Categories show the thumbnail an admin uploads on the category's Media card: as photo tiles on Search before typing, contained on the product tile and never cropped, with a plain mist tile until one is uploaded. Home's category chips stay names.
  • Search matches product names only, so it has no filters or sort. Shops rank by distance band (under 5, 5–15, over 15 km), then by how fast they ship, and distances show in whole kilometres so no shop can be pinpointed (ADR 0011).
  • Reviews show an average and count, with no star breakdown or photos. Reject reasons are free text; cancelling an accepted order picks one of four reasons, with a note for "Other".
  • There are no delivery areas: every courier delivers nationwide, and checkout finds a shop it can't deliver from by its courier rates. Perishable items go only with couriers arriving by the end of the next day, otherwise pickup.

Corners, controls and hierarchy

  • Corners: 8 chips and small tiles · 12 rows, inputs, product tiles · 16 cards and buttons · 20 sheets' inner cards · 24 sheets and the tab bar · full for pills and avatars.
  • Buttons: 52 sticky main action · 48 standard · 44 inside a list row. Every hit area is at least 44pt.
  • One title per screen, sized by role: tab root 26, a screen named after a thing (shop, product) 22, pushed screens 20. Never two display sizes competing at the top.
  • Order of emphasis on every screen: title, then the one primary action (indigo, bottom), then section headings (18), then row titles (13–14 bold), then meta (11.5 muted). Colour is reserved for state, never for emphasis alone.
  • Grouping: space first (tight inside a group, 20–24 between groups), then a light divider for lists, never boxes inside boxes.
Foundations

The values to build from, in device points

The screens are drawn at mockup scale: a 393pt iPhone screen is 282px wide, so 1 mockup px = 1.394pt. Every value below is already converted, so nobody measures a mockup. They become the tokens in packages/ui/src/tokens.ts, and screens use tokens only, never raw values. Items marked Proposed aren't drawn in the mockups; they're the recommended default until agreed. Items marked Decision need a choice before the build.

Scale: check on a device first Decision

  • At 1.394, body text is 18pt and the sticky main button is 72pt tall. iOS's own defaults are 17pt and about 50pt. That fits the AAA target and the vendor app's counter use, but it's large for the buyer app.
  • Before the tokens are locked, put Home, Product and the vendor Orders list on a real phone at this scale. Then either keep it, or apply one factor to every size so the proportions stay the same.

Elevation

  • Light: flat. Surfaces are separated by a 1pt line or a tile fill, never by shadows.
  • Dark: lighter greys instead of shadows: bg, then raised, then tile.
  • Floating only: the map's locate button and pin, and the save heart on a photo, get a soft shadow (y 4, blur 12, black at 15%; the heart y 1, blur 3). Nothing else floats.
  • A scrim behind sheets: black at 60%.

Fonts

  • Bundle Bricolage Grotesque (700, 800) and Manrope (400, 600, 700, 800) with expo-font, which both apps already have. Both fonts are under the SIL Open Font License.
  • Android needs one family name per weight (Manrope_700Bold), so map each token to a family, not to fontWeight.
  • Prices and codes use tabular figures (fontVariant: ['tabular-nums']), so totals line up.

Icons: Lucide

Every icon comes from Lucide through lucide-react-native, which needs react-native-svg. Neither app has either package yet. The drawn glyphs already follow Lucide's grid: 24-unit box, outline, round caps and joins. So the build swaps each one for its Lucide match, in the same place and at the same weight.

  • Sizes: 24pt by default (drawn 18), 22pt in add buttons (16), 20pt in steps (14), 16pt in chips (12).
  • Stroke: strokeWidth 1.8 by default, 2.4 in add buttons and 2.2 in chips, as drawn. Lucide's own default is 2. Don't set absoluteStrokeWidth, so the stroke scales with the size, as it does in the mockups.
  • Colour: color takes a colour token, usually ink, muted or onPrimary. Filled states (a saved heart, rating stars) set fill to the same token.
  • One Icon component in @oja/ui wraps Lucide, so size, stroke and colour come from tokens, and icon-only buttons always carry a spoken label.

Motion Proposed

  • Drawn: the new-order alarm ring pulses every 1.6s, ease-out, scaling from 0.92 to 1.3 as it fades. The skeleton shimmer sweeps every 1.4s, ease-in-out. The spinner turns once every 0.9s.
  • Sheets (confirm steps, Order again, Not available) open with the platform's own sheet presentation (Expo Router formSheet), not a custom animation.
  • Press: feedback shows at once and fades over 100ms. Buttons don't change size.
  • Tabs and segments: the underline slides over 200ms, ease-out, and the content cross-fades over 150ms.
  • Lists: a row added or removed (an accepted order, a ticked line) animates its layout over 200ms.
  • Takeover: fades in over 250ms, full screen.
  • Toasts slide up over 200ms and leave after 4s.
  • Reduce Motion: no slides or pulses. Everything cross-fades, and the alarm ring holds still.

Haptics Proposed

Uses expo-haptics, which neither app has yet.

  • Success: order placed, order accepted, packed, handed over, collected, review posted.
  • Selection: a line ticked or scanned on the pick list, a quantity stepped, an option chosen.
  • Warning: a barcode that matches no line, a pickup code that's wrong.
  • New order: a repeating heavy pattern paired with the alarm sound, until Accept, View order or Not now.
  • None: on ordinary navigation, scrolling or tab switches.

Phone sizes and text size Proposed

  • Baseline: 393pt wide (iPhone 15 and 16). Layouts stretch to fill wider phones (430pt); there's no maximum width. Tablets are out of scope for v1.
  • Small phones (375pt, and Android at 360dp): the gutter stays 24pt, the product grid stays two columns with narrower tiles, and rails keep scrolling. Below 375pt, the vendor stage tiles go from 4 across to 2 by 2.
  • Larger system text: text follows the phone's text-size setting. Rows grow taller, and names, prices and chips wrap instead of truncating. Buttons grow with their label. Display sizes (28pt and up) scale to at most 1.3×. The tab bar follows the platform's own limit.
  • Respect safe areas on every edge. The sticky main action sits above the home indicator.

Android, and the tab bar

  • On Android, values are in dp, the same numbers as pt. The layout runs edge to edge under transparent system bars. The system back gesture does what the header's back button does, and press feedback is a ripple.
  • The buyer app uses native tabs (NativeTabs); the vendor app is a stack with no tab bar, as today: Liquid Glass on iOS 26 and Material on Android. Native tabs can't take the drawn floating bar's 24pt corners and custom fill, and they draw their icons from SF Symbols and Material Symbols, not Lucide. Decided: keep native tabs. The five tab icons are the platform's; every other icon is Lucide. The drawn tab bar shows order, labels and icons only.

Tile rules

  • Every product tile is white in Light and charcoal (#26262A) in Dark, with no outline and no veil. Spacing separates the tiles (12 between columns, 16 between rows), and the price and name below anchor each one. The image is contained with a margin of about 10%, never cropped to fill.
  • Buttons, badges and carousel dots sit over the photo on the same tile. The add button keeps a 44pt hit area in code.
  • Vendor and Open Food Facts photos show whole, framed by the tile. The quick-add tip ("square, plain background, whole product in view") nudges new photos towards the pack-shot look.
  • The pickup QR and shop logos sit on white in both themes, and shop banners show as uploaded.
Dark mode

Neutral after dark, indigo where it means something

Nine screens that carry the most weight, in dark mode. Surfaces are neutral charcoal with no brand tint, so indigo does the same jobs it does in light mode: the brand band, primary buttons, selection and links. Against a neutral page, the band reads as a branded surface rather than more navy. Product photos are cut-outs on a charcoal tile, and the pickup QR stays on white so scanners can read it.

Rules for dark

  • Leaf, ember and red chips switch to deep backgrounds with light text. Quantity pills keep their light-mode fills, which still pass with white text.
  • Sun stays the same in both themes, always with dark text.
  • Surfaces are neutral greys with no brand tint, and elevation comes from lighter greys, not shadows: page, then cards, then tiles.
  • The new-order takeover is the same indigo in both themes. At night it is the brightest thing on the counter.
  • Follow the phone by default. Appearance (buyer Account, vendor Settings) chooses System, Light or Dark on this phone; Light and Dark hold whatever the phone is set to, including Clerk, sheets, the tab bar, headers and the status bar (#102).
Accessibility audit

Built to WCAG 2.2 AAA

The target is WCAG 2.2 Level AAA, the highest level, in both themes. Every text pair is at 7:1 or more, every control outline at 3:1 or more, and every touch target at least 44pt. Ratios are computed from the token values. The mockups draw a 393pt iPhone screen 282px wide, so 1px here is about 1.4pt on a device. Where a check failed, the screens above have already been changed.

AAA needed one thing the app doesn't have yet: a phone-only setting that turns the full-screen new-order alert into a banner (3.2.5). It needs no backend change. Everything else is colour, size, copy and how the existing screens behave.

Buyer app

Arrive

Browsing is open to guests. Sign-in comes when it's needed, and a guest cart carries over.

Home, full scroll

The whole page for a returning buyer in Surulere, top to bottom, with the fold marked. It needs the new shops route; everything else on it exists.

What each part is, and where its data comes from

  1. Band. The delivery address the buyer chose, their first name from their account, and search. delivering-to · Clerk · GET /store/listings?q=
  2. Category chips. The store's top-level categories, as names; their thumbnails show on Search's category tiles instead. useCategories
  3. Near you. A page of shops, each with its logo (or monogram), distance, open state with today's closing time, and its top products. Shops rank by distance band, then ship time, then distance, so a nearer shop can follow one in the same band that ships faster; distances are whole kilometres. Sale prices show crossed out, and a shop's automatic promotion shows as its "10% off at this shop" badge. A sold-out product shows "Sold out" in place of its add button. new GET /store/shops, ranked in the database · closes_at · seller.logo · offerPrice · dealLabel
  4. End of list. More shops load as the buyer scrolls; after the last one, a way to change the address. Every live shop in the region is listed: couriers deliver nationwide, so there is no delivery area. shops paging

Band and status bar

Loading, errors and empty states

Every state the apps already handle, drawn once. Loading shows the shape of what's coming; errors say what happened, what's safe and one way to retry; empty states say what will appear and how to get there.

State rules

  • Loading: skeletons in the shape of the content, never a blank screen. Anything known on the phone (address, greeting, shop name, back button) shows immediately. A spinner only for waits with no layout (confirming a payment, opening Paystack).
  • Refreshing: pull to refresh keeps the current list on screen; a failed refresh shows a short toast, not an error screen.
  • Errors: a quiet mist emblem, a plain-language title from the code ("Couldn't load your orders"), what is safe ("You haven't been charged", "Anything in your cart is saved"), and Try again. Raw error messages go to logs, not the screen.
  • Empty: the indigo pattern emblem, what will appear here, and at most two ways forward.
  • Inline problems (a payment not completed, an address that didn't save, a price that changed) sit above the button they affect, in the ember note, and are announced to screen readers.
