Rules that keep it disciplined

  • The adire pattern appears in brand moments only: sign-in, the Home band, shop and order headers, pickup codes, confirmations and the vendor app's headers. It always fades out behind text, so letters never sit on its strokes.
  • Every full-screen surface is indigo or white. Green, ember and red stay at chip, pill and badge size; red also marks destructive buttons.
  • One primary indigo button per screen, carrying the price when money moves.
  • Status chips always pair a symbol and a label with colour.
  • Photos sit on mist tiles, square, uncropped. Vendors get a photo standard: square, plain background.
  • Errors say what happened and what to do next, in the shopper's words.

Built only on what exists

  • Every screen uses data the buyer app, vendor app and backend already have. Nothing here needs a new endpoint.
  • Deals are shown the way the backend has them: a shop's sale price crossed out, and its automatic promotion as a badge. There's no deals feed or countdown, because sale end dates don't reach the buyer.
  • Shops and categories have no images, so shops use monograms and the pattern, and categories are name chips.
  • Search matches product names only, so it has no filters or sort. Listings come nearest first.
  • Reviews show an average and count, with no star breakdown or photos. Reject reasons are free text, as the vendor app has them today.

Corners, controls and hierarchy

  • Corners: 8 chips and small tiles · 12 rows, inputs, product tiles · 16 cards and buttons · 20 sheets' inner cards · 24 sheets and the tab bar · full for pills and avatars.
  • Buttons: 52 sticky main action · 48 standard · 44 inside a list row. Every hit area is at least 44pt.
  • One title per screen, sized by role: tab root 26, a screen named after a thing (shop, product) 22, pushed screens 20. Never two display sizes competing at the top.
  • Order of emphasis on every screen: title, then the one primary action (indigo, bottom), then section headings (18), then row titles (13–14 bold), then meta (11.5 muted). Colour is reserved for state, never for emphasis alone.
  • Grouping: space first (tight inside a group, 20–24 between groups), then a light divider for lists, never boxes inside boxes.
Product images

Built for the images we will actually get

Product images reach Oja three ways. Catalogue imports bring commercial pack shots, almost always on pure white. Some arrive as cutouts with transparent backgrounds. Open Food Facts and vendors' quick-add photos are real photos with real backgrounds. The import copies images as they are; background removal is planned in the design doc but not built. So the tile has to look right with all three, untouched.

Mist tile, photo fills itAs drawn until now. White pack shots become white squares that don't match their neighbours, cutouts float on lilac, and vendor photos are cropped.Rejected
Mist tile, photo containedNothing is cropped, but every white pack shot shows as a white box inside a tinted square.Rejected
White tile, contained with a margin, no outlineWhite pack shots disappear into the tile, so every source looks consistent. Cutouts sit on white, and vendor photos keep their whole frame. Nothing needs processing.Chosen
The same, in dark modeTiles stay white in dark mode, as most large shops do, so product colours stay true. A charcoal tile would turn every white pack shot into a glowing box.Chosen

Tile rules

  • Every product tile is white with no outline, in both themes. Spacing separates the tiles (12 between columns, 16 between rows), and the price and name below anchor each one. The image is contained with a margin of about 10%, never cropped to fill.
  • On a white page, a white pack shot's tile edge disappears, so the product itself is the shape. Tiles still have a rounded 44pt-plus tap area in code.
  • Vendor and Open Food Facts photos show whole, framed by the tile. That makes their mixed backgrounds look intended. The quick-add tip ("square, plain background, whole product in view") nudges new photos towards the pack-shot look.
  • If background removal is built later, cutouts drop onto the same white tile and nothing else changes.
Dark mode

Neutral after dark, indigo where it means something

Nine screens that carry the most weight, in dark mode. Surfaces are neutral charcoal with no brand tint, so indigo does the same jobs it does in light mode: the brand band, primary buttons, selection and links. Against a neutral page, the band reads as a branded surface rather than more navy. Product photos keep their own light backgrounds, and the pickup QR stays on white so scanners can read it.

Rules for dark

  • Leaf, ember and red chips switch to deep backgrounds with light text. Quantity pills keep their light-mode fills, which still pass with white text.
  • Sun stays the same in both themes, always with dark text.
  • Surfaces are neutral greys with no brand tint, and elevation comes from lighter greys, not shadows: page, then cards, then tiles.
  • The new-order takeover is the same indigo in both themes. At night it is the brightest thing on the counter.
  • Follow the system setting, with no in-app toggle in v1.
Accessibility audit

Built to WCAG 2.2 AAA

The target is WCAG 2.2 Level AAA, the highest level, in both themes. Every text pair is at 7:1 or more, every control outline at 3:1 or more, and every touch target at least 44pt. Ratios are computed from the token values. The mockups draw a 393pt iPhone screen 282px wide, so 1px here is about 1.4pt on a device. Where a check failed, the screens above have already been changed.

AAA needed one thing the app doesn't have yet: a phone-only setting that turns the full-screen new-order alert into a banner (3.2.5). It needs no backend change. Everything else is colour, size, copy and how the existing screens behave.

Buyer app

Arrive

Browsing is open to guests. Sign-in comes when it's needed, and a guest cart carries over.

Home, full scroll

The whole page for a returning buyer in Surulere, top to bottom, with the fold marked. Everything on it comes from what the app and backend already do.

What each part is, and where its data comes from

  1. Band. The delivery address the buyer chose, their first name from their account, and search. delivering-to · Clerk · GET /store/listings?q=
  2. Category chips. The store's top-level categories, as names: categories carry no images. useCategories
  3. Near you. Today's Home list, regrouped by shop. Listings already arrive grouped by shop, nearest first, with each shop's distance and open or closed state. Sale prices show crossed out, and a shop's automatic promotion shows as its "10% off at this shop" badge. GET /store/listings · offerPrice · dealLabel
  4. End of list. The last shop that delivers to this address, then a way to change the address. listings paging

For a first-time buyer or a guest

  • The band asks for an address instead of greeting by name, and the address sheet opens on first launch.
  • Without an address, listings aren't ranked by distance and the distance is left off each shop.

Left out because nothing supports it yet

  • A deals section with a countdown: sale end dates stay inside the shop's price list and never reach the buyer, and listings can't be filtered to "on sale".
  • A "Buy again" rail, "market staples" and a featured shop on Home. Order again lives on delivered orders instead (After purchase); a Home rail can follow once that proves useful.
  • Shop cover photos and category images: shops and categories carry no images.

Product, full scroll

Everything the product screen holds, top to bottom, with the fold marked. Add to cart stays pinned to the bottom while the page scrolls.

What each part is, and where its data comes from

  1. Photos. Every image the product has, swiped sideways, with dots for position. Save sits in the header. product.images · wishlist toggle
  2. Summary. Name, this shop's price (a sale shows crossed out, with the shop's deal badge), the shop and its rating, and quantity. A closed shop shows when it reopens, and Add to cart is disabled. offerPrice · ShopRating · ShopStatusBadge
  3. Size. One chip per variant this shop offers; the price and button follow the choice. Hidden when there's only one. offers[].variant_title
  4. Delivery and pickup. The shop's best-value courier date and fee to the buyer's address, or its dispatch time ("Ships in 2 days") when there's no address yet. Pickup shows only where the shop has a collection point. useDeliveryEstimate · leadtime_to_ship
  5. Description. The catalogue description, when there is one. product.description
  6. Details. The category's attributes and the weight, as label and value. product.attributes · weight
  7. Reviews. The product's average, count and latest reviews, then "See all". Hidden until it has reviews. useReviewPreview
  8. Credit. Shown only for data from a source whose licence requires it. product.attribution
  9. More from this shop. Other products from the same shop, as small cards. Left out when there are none. shop suggestions

  • The Paystack and refund note is gone from this screen. Payment and refunds are explained at checkout, where they matter.

Orders, full scroll

The Ongoing tab for a buyer with a few weeks of orders, top to bottom, with the fold marked. The Past tab follows.

What each part is, and where its data comes from

  1. One row per checkout. A checkout with one shop is titled with the shop; one split across shops says "2 shops" and lists each shop's state underneath. Newest first. GET /store/order-history · historyEntries
  2. Date, items and order number. When it was placed, how many items, and the number the shop and Oja's support use. created_at · items.quantity · display_id
  3. Photos. The first items' thumbnails, so a buyer finds an order by what was in it. items.thumbnail
  4. Status chips. Each shop's own state, in the same words as the order: Waiting for the shop, Packed, On its way, Ready for pickup, Delivered, Collected, Rejected by the shop, Refunded. Anything waiting on the buyer is ember. OrderStatusChips
  5. Order again. Replaces the total on a delivered or collected order (the total moves to the meta line). Opens the Order again sheet. designed, not built
  6. Ongoing and Past tabs. Ongoing holds anything not finished: waiting for the shop, being prepared, packed, on its way, ready for pickup, or waiting on the buyer (like a refund that needs bank details); a checkout split across shops stays here until every shop is done. Past holds delivered, collected, rejected, cancelled and refunded, with Order again and search by item. Each tab shows its count; the screen opens on Ongoing when anything is in progress, otherwise on Past. needs GET /store/order-history?state=active|past and ?q=, not built
  7. End of list. Ongoing is short and loads whole. Past loads more as the buyer scrolls. limit · offset

Orders and order history are the same list

  • The Orders tab is the buyer's whole order history: active orders sit at the top because they're newest, finished ones follow. There is no separate history screen.
  • Tapping a row opens that checkout's order detail, with each shop's part.
  • Pull to refresh; it also refreshes itself every minute while open.

Loading, errors and empty states

Every state the apps already handle, drawn once. Loading shows the shape of what's coming; errors say what happened, what's safe and one way to retry; empty states say what will appear and how to get there.

State rules

  • Loading: skeletons in the shape of the content, never a blank screen. Anything known on the phone (address, greeting, shop name, back button) shows immediately. A spinner only for waits with no layout (confirming a payment, opening Paystack).
  • Refreshing: pull to refresh keeps the current list on screen; a failed refresh shows a short toast, not an error screen.
  • Errors: a quiet mist emblem, a plain-language title from the code ("Couldn't load your orders"), what is safe ("You haven't been charged", "Anything in your cart is saved"), and Try again. Raw error messages go to logs, not the screen.
  • Empty: the indigo pattern emblem, what will appear here, and at most two ways forward.
  • Inline problems (a payment not completed, an address that didn't save, a price that changed) sit above the button they affect, in the ember note, and are announced to screen readers.
